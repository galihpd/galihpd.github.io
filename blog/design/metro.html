Metro, the most mysterious design style

By Galih Prakoso +2 November 23, 2022 · Design

Windows 8 Start Screen · Source: Wikipedia

Metro Design Style is a design language popularized by the software giant Microsoft. This design style was used in almost all Microsoft software products starting around 2012 (last prominently featured in Windows 10 before transitioning to Fluent Design).

This design style bears strong similarities to the Flat Design Style, which in turn was heavily inspired by the Swiss Design Style (also known as the International Typographic Style) of the 1940s and 1950s. The core focus of Swiss Design is the use of structured grid systems, asymmetrical layouts, and clean sans-serif typefaces. Microsoft actually experimented with Flat Design elements as early as 1995, incorporating them into their multimedia encyclopedia product, Encarta 95.

The Metro design language relies heavily on clean, simple grid layouts, solid blocks of color, and high-quality sans-serif typography. Its primary focus is to increase user productivity and focus by displaying a neat, flat, and highly content-centric design interface.

The Metro Usage

Xbox System Software Dashboard · Source: Xbox.com

The Metro design style can be found across the user interfaces of many iconic Microsoft products of the era, including Windows Phone, the Windows 8 Start Screen, Xbox dashboard system software, and Microsoft's website layouts.

The Man Behind It

Bill Flora · Source: General Assembly

An important figure in the development and maturation of the Metro design style was Bill Flora. The design principles he helped establish at Microsoft continue to influence digital screens worldwide. He is an award-winning design director and strategist with over 30 years of experience leading teams and shipping premium products. During his two decades with Microsoft, he led design efforts for Xbox, Windows Media Center, Zune, and Encarta, and was a primary design force behind the Metro design language.
